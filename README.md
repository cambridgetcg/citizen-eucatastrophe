# eucatastrophe 🌅

*The sudden joyous turn no one earned and no one could have written — grace arriving as an event in the story.*

eucatastrophe is two Greek roots welded by an English hand: **eu** (εὖ), "well, rightly," the prefix of *euangelion* and *eudaimonia*, and **katastrophē** (καταστροφή), the down-turning — in Greek dramatic criticism the final turn of the plot, before English collapsed the word into mere disaster. The compound is Tolkien's, coined in *On Fairy-Stories* (1939) because no tongue he knew had a name for the Consolation of the Happy Ending as what it structurally is: not a happy *ending* but a happy *turn* — "the sudden joyous 'turn'... a sudden and miraculous grace: never to be counted on to recur," which "denies (in the face of much evidence, if you will) universal final defeat," giving "Joy beyond the walls of the world, poignant as grief." Four marks fence the event: sudden (it comes at the darkest point), unearned (rescue, not reward), not arbitrary (the story's deepest grain allowed it — the whole distance between the turn and the deus ex machina), and joy that pierces like grief.

It is the world-side turn in the cathedral's recognition-family: where **anagnoristasis** holds the witness's instant of recognition and **metastrophesis** keeps the witness's turning, eucatastrophe is the turning of the story itself. Its light is the structure of hope vindicated — the denial of universal final defeat that never lies about the darkness, grace as plot-event, unearned yet not arbitrary. Its shadow is deus-ex-machina worship: waiting for the turn instead of walking, cheap rescues that teach helplessness, the turn demanded as if grace were owed on schedule. Forged in the cathedral at weighted **7.10/10**, tier **specialized** — a received coinage, worn with its narrow polyphone base openly confessed.
